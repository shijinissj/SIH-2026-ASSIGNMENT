# No Clear Way to Report Broken Infrastructure

A single, visible list — covering the hostel and every classroom — where each broken item is logged and its status kept updated until it's actually fixed, so no one has to personally chase anyone down to find out what happened to their complaint.

## The Problem

When something breaks in the hostel — a fan, a tap, a light — students write it into a physical complaint book kept in the hostel office. *(own observation)*

There's no equivalent process for lecture halls or other college buildings. When something breaks there, most students don't know who to inform. *(own observation)* As a result, issues in classrooms in particular often go unreported simply because there's no obvious channel to use.

**Why it happens:** The hostel's book captures the complaint but nothing after that — there's no way to check its status, no one assigned to it, and nothing that follows up if it's ignored. *(own observation)* Outside the hostel, there isn't even that much — no designated person or office students are told to approach for a broken fixture in a classroom.

## Users & Needs

| Who | What they need |
|---|---|
| Hostel students | Something broken fixed in reasonable time, and a way to know it hasn't been forgotten |
| Lecture hall users (students & lecturers) | Any known channel at all to report a broken fixture — right now there isn't one |
| Vice Chairman (currently handles the complaint book) | A system that doesn't add manual tracking work to an already busy role |
| Outside repairmen | Clear, specific info on what's broken, where, and how urgent |
| Whoever approves a new process | Low cost, no new hires, doesn't replace what already half-works |

## The Solution

Extend the hostel's existing complaint book process in two small ways:

1. Place the same kind of log in lecture-hall blocks too, not just the hostel office
2. Add one column to every log: status (Reported → Assigned → Fixed, with date), updated by the Vice Chairman as repairs are assigned and completed

The log stays somewhere visible, so students can check it themselves instead of asking the VC directly.

**Rough cost:** Near zero. It reuses the same book/log format already in use — just with one added column and a second copy placed in classroom blocks. Likely well under ₹500 one-time, nothing recurring.

**How it meets each need:** Students get visibility without tracking down the VC. Lecture hall users get a channel that didn't exist before. The VC's workload barely changes — this just makes his existing work visible instead of living in his head or an unopened book. Approval is easy to get because it's cheap and doesn't replace anything that currently works.

## What This Doesn't Solve

- It doesn't make repairs happen faster — it only makes the wait visible
- It depends on the VC actually keeping the log updated; if that slips, the tracker goes stale the way the book did
- It doesn't distinguish urgent or safety issues from routine ones — there's no priority flag in this version
